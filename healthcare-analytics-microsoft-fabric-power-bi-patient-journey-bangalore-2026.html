The 23-Step Patient Journey: What Analytics Actually Tracks

Before understanding the technology, you need to understand what a patient journey looks like through an analytics lens. It's not just "admission → treatment → discharge." It's 23 measurable touchpoints across digital, clinical, and financial systems:

Stage 1: Digital Discovery & Booking (Touchpoints 1-4)

The patient's journey often starts before they enter the hospital — with a Google search, the hospital website, or a teleconsultation app.

Fabric's Lakehouse Architecture for Healthcare

Using the Bronze-Silver-Gold pattern in Fabric's Lakehouse, the analytics team creates a single patient journey table that joins data from all 9 systems:

fabric_lakehouse_healthcare — Silver layer 
-- Unified Patient Journey Table (Silver)
SELECT
    e.patient_id,
    e.visit_id,
    e.arrival_timestamp,
    r.reg_wait_minutes,              -- from HMS
    c.doctor_id,
    c.department,
    c.diagnosis_icd10,                -- from EMR
    l.lab_tests_ordered,
    l.lab_results_time_hours,        -- from LIS
    p.radiology_wait_hours,          -- from RIS/PACS
    s.surgery_scheduled,
    s.surgery_actual_start,           -- from OT scheduling
    ph.pharmacy_dispense_minutes,     -- from Pharmacy
    b.total_bill_amount,
    b.insurance_claim_status,        -- from Billing
    i.preauth_wait_hours,            -- from Insurance
    f.nps_score,                      -- from Patient Portal
    d.discharge_timestamp,
    DATEDIFF(hour, e.arrival_timestamp, d.discharge_timestamp) AS total_journey_hours,
    CASE WHEN readmit.visit_id IS NOT NULL THEN 1 ELSE 0 END   AS readmitted_30day
FROM bronze_emr e
LEFT JOIN bronze_hms r    ON e.visit_id = r.visit_id
LEFT JOIN bronze_lis l    ON e.visit_id = l.visit_id
-- ... 6 more joins

This single table — with 25+ columns from 9 systems — is the foundation for every dashboard, every KPI, and every predictive model. It's what makes patient journey analytics possible.

The Skills You Need for Healthcare Analytics in Bangalore

Healthcare analytics doesn't require a medical degree. It requires data skills + domain understanding. Here's the stack:

01

SQL — The Foundation

Joining EMR with HMS with LIS data requires advanced SQL — JOINs, window functions for LOS calculations, CTEs for complex patient journey queries. Every healthcare analyst writes SQL daily.

02

Power BI + DAX — Dashboarding

Healthcare KPIs require complex DAX: ALOS (average length of stay using AVERAGEX), bed occupancy (DISTINCTCOUNT at a point-in-time), readmission rate (CALCULATE + FILTER + date logic), NPS trends (time intelligence).

03

Microsoft Fabric — Data Engineering

Ingesting HL7/FHIR messages, building Bronze-Silver-Gold lakehouse, managing patient data pipelines. Healthcare data is messy — Fabric handles the complexity that raw SQL can't.

04

Python/ML — Predictive Models

Building readmission, no-show, and LOS prediction models using scikit-learn/XGBoost in Fabric's Spark notebooks. Not required for entry-level, but a massive differentiator for mid-level roles.

05

Healthcare Domain Knowledge

Understanding ICD-10 codes, NABH metrics (ALOS, bed turnover, infection rate), healthcare data standards (HL7, FHIR), insurance workflows (TPA, pre-auth, claim submission). This is what separates general analysts from healthcare analysts.

FAQ

Healthcare Analytics FAQ

What is patient journey mapping in healthcare analytics?

Patient journey mapping in healthcare analytics tracks every interaction a patient has with a hospital system — from first appointment booking to post-discharge follow-up. It includes digital touchpoints (app/website), clinical touchpoints (OPD, diagnostics, surgery, pharmacy), and financial touchpoints (billing, insurance). Analytics teams use tools like Microsoft Fabric to unify data from all these systems and Power BI to visualize the complete journey, identify bottlenecks, and improve patient experience and outcomes.

How is Microsoft Fabric used in healthcare analytics?

Microsoft Fabric unifies healthcare data from disparate systems — EMR/EHR, hospital management systems (HMS), lab information systems (LIS), pharmacy systems, billing, and insurance — into a single platform using OneLake. Data Factory pipelines ingest and transform clinical and operational data, Data Science workloads build predictive models (readmission risk, no-show prediction, length of stay), and Power BI delivers real-time dashboards to hospital leadership, department heads, and clinical staff.

What Power BI dashboards do hospitals use?

Hospitals use multiple Power BI dashboards: (1) Executive KPI dashboard — bed occupancy, ALOS, revenue, patient satisfaction; (2) Patient flow dashboard — real-time bed status, wait times, queue lengths; (3) Clinical outcomes dashboard — readmission rates, complication rates, mortality indices; (4) Financial dashboard — revenue by department, insurance claim status, AR aging; (5) Patient journey dashboard — end-to-end flow from booking to discharge with bottleneck identification.

Do I need a medical background for healthcare analytics?

No. You need data skills (SQL, Power BI, Fabric) and a willingness to learn healthcare domain concepts (ICD-10 codes, NABH metrics, HL7/FHIR standards). Domain knowledge is taught on the job — no hospital expects a data analyst to be a doctor. However, having basic healthcare literacy (understanding what ALOS means, how insurance pre-auth works, what readmission is) gives you a significant edge in interviews. What are the key healthcare KPIs I should know for interviews?

The 8 KPIs every hospital analytics team tracks: (1) Bed Occupancy Rate = (Occupied beds / Total beds) × 100; (2) ALOS (Average Length of Stay) = Total patient days / Total discharges; (3) Bed Turnover Rate = Total discharges / Average beds; (4) Readmission Rate (30-day) = Readmissions within 30 days / Total discharges; (5) OPD Wait Time = Time from registration to doctor consultation; (6) Lab TAT (Turnaround Time) = Time from sample collection to report; (7) NPS (Net Promoter Score) = % Promoters - % Detractors; (8) Claim Rejection Rate = Rejected claims / Total claims.
